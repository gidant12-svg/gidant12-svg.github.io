My First Investment, a 20% Loss, and a Letter from a Financial Demon

⏱ 5 min read November 2026 Share on LinkedIn
GA
Gideon AnthonyEquity research · CFA Level II candidate

My first ever stock investment was IonQ. I know, a very peculiar choice. For context, IonQ is a quantum computing company I had discovered earlier in April, as I chased the prestige of becoming a quantum computing analyst. Well, this was long before I learnt of a thing called analyst bias.

You know, when you pick up a stock and you think you just KNOW that the stock will perform well. Because surely, as a new grad with virtually no experience analysing stocks, I could see the hidden tells of the market that even seasoned analysts couldn't see.

Well, fast forward two weeks. -20%. A jaw-dropping, fear-inducing shock, as my jaw practically dislocated and my mouth fell to the ground like an anaconda preparing to devour prey ten times its size.

That -20% in red truly opened my eyes to the reality of what I was getting myself into. (I'm glad I'm early in my career, so it was a relatively capital-light investment.) I promptly accepted my losses, salvaged what remained of my investment, and plugged the scavenged remains into ETFs. Having tasted real losses, perhaps my naivety of being a risk lover died that day.

Two months later, IonQ recovered from the loss. Of course it did. Maybe I'd made the right call originally, or perhaps my lack of patience got the better of me. Then again, maybe that's just hindsight bias talking. But I learnt a valuable lesson: patience is a virtue.

Now, I'm telling this story not as some great epiphany, but because I recently read a transcript of VALUE: After Hours (S07 E26): Magnetic Position Sensors $MELE, Promo Market $FOUR and Ingles $IMKTA, featuring Tobias Carlisle, Jake Taylor and Gwen Hofmeyr. In it, Jake Taylor attempts something rather peculiar: a financial adaptation of C. S. Lewis's The Screwtape Letters. Essentially, a senior financial demon writes to his eager nephew, advising him on how to ruin an investor.

I read this, and albeit long, I found myself in it. I'd recommend reading it, particularly if you're in a similar position. Or even if you're not. I reckon you might just find yourself in it.

A Financial Screwtape Letter — Jake Taylor

Written and performed by Jake Taylor on VALUE: After Hours, Season 7, Episode 26.
An original financial satire inspired by C. S. Lewis's The Screwtape Letters (1942). Reproduced with permission from Jake Taylor.

My dearest, Tick Worm,

I read your latest report with something approaching satisfaction. Your patient, I gather, has opened a brokerage account, downloaded two trading apps, opened a crypto wallet and started referring to himself without irony as early-stage capital. Excellent, you now stand at the edge of your first real assignment. Not merely to ruin him, but to ruin him in the most elegant way possible, through the rituals of self-diluted financial sophistication.

We do not wish to keep him away from markets. That's outdated thinking. The more delicious path is to let him enter fully, but wrongly. Let him study enough to be confident, but never enough to be correct. Begin with speculation disguised as investment. Encourage him to chase stories, not actual financial statements. Momentum stocks, SPACs with vaporists, forecasts, hot tips that feel like privileged information, anything trending on social feeds. He must believe he's early to the next wave, though he's simply the inevitable bag holder arriving for the rug pull.

Let him believe risk is merely a byproduct of cowardice. Caution, you'll whisper to him, is a tax on ambition. You only need to get rich once, so why not do it quickly, boldly and with an extra helping of excitement? A man of his caliber deserves good stories to woo the opposite sex, not boring business fundamentals and monk like patience.

Next, initiate him into the cult of macro-obsession. Whisper to him that every CPI release, every Fed meeting and dot plot, and every unemployment print is a tactical moment. He must trade on this data. Let him believe he's interpreting signals that even the professionals misread. Was that a twitch in Powell's eyebrow that only he saw? The market isn't ignoring him, it's simply not enlightened yet. He's ahead of the curve.

And now, the algorithm will deliver him to one of our favorite weapons, the TikTok Financial influencer. Let him follow 21-year-old prophets who preach conviction while leaning against rented sports cars. This coin will 10x they declare and your patient obeys. He's not reading, he is scrolling. He's not evaluating, he's imitating. This is what we call user generated misfortune. And it's a favorite of our Dark Fathers.

From here, elevate him into options trading, where ignorance and leverage embrace in holy combustion. He will say Gamma and Theta as incantations while sacrificing premiums weekly. When he wins, let him feel chosen and smarter than all the other dumb money neophytes. When he loses, tell him he was simply early. We want him addicted to the idea of mastery while remaining functionally confused.

Finally, when he grows weary, offer him the false comfort of diversification. He must not know what he really owns as he scatters his holdings across crypto, uranium, distressed real estate, leveraged ETFs, money losing SPACs, pointless NFTs and vintage wine startups. This, he will say is strategy, but it is clutter and it is our free lunch to his torment.

The high fee burdens of these complex instruments help ensure his road to ruin. Let him spend a decade in motion and call it growth. Let him perform diligence without discipline, conviction without clarity and action without understanding. The delicious tragedy is not that he will eventually be financially devastated. It is that he will think he was smarter than average the whole time. That eventual bitterness of his broken spirit is the sweetest fruit for a financial demon.

And by all means, never let him write anything down, lest he learn from his mistakes. Steer him away from systemization that will lead to self-awareness and an inevitable improvement. Keep the idea of a businesslike approach far from his mind. That is too boring and pedestrian for someone as smart as our man.

Yours an infinite churn,
Abyssmuss.
